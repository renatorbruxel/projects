Analytical Framework

How I Think About GTM Analytics

Most GTM dashboards report what happened. The gap to elite RevOps is building tools that predict what will happen and prescribe what to change. That requires a layered analytical architecture.

1

Operational Layer

Is the machine running? Activity, hygiene, submission compliance, data quality. Necessary but not sufficient for strategic decisions.

Hygiene scores · Activity metrics · Submission rates · Field completeness
2

Performance Layer

Is the machine producing? Attainment, pipeline coverage, forecast accuracy, velocity. What most RevOps teams stop at.

Quota attainment · 3x coverage · Win rates · Sales cycle length
3

Strategic Layer

Is the machine building a durable advantage? ICP quality compounding, NRR, TAM penetration, GTM efficiency. Where differentiation lives.

Magic Number · LTV:CAC · NRR cohorts · TAM penetration · ICP win rate

Every metric needs a decision

If you can't name the action that changes when the metric changes, cut it. A metric without a decision is a number without a purpose.

Reference

Metrics Glossary

Canonical definitions for every key metric in this portfolio — formula, business interpretation, inclusion/exclusion rules, and known caveats. This is the operational contract that makes dashboards trustworthy.

Pipeline Velocity ($/day)
(# Opps × Avg ACV × Win Rate) ÷ Avg Cycle Days

Interpretation: Dollars of closed revenue generated per selling day. The four-lever decomposition (volume, ACV, win rate, cycle) isolates the source of any degradation. Caveat: Must be segmented — blending Enterprise and SMB produces a meaningless average.

Magic Number (GM-adjusted)
(Net New ARR × Gross Margin %) ÷ Prior Quarter S&M Spend

Benchmarks: >1.0 = scale aggressively | 0.75–1.0 = grow thoughtfully | 0.5–0.75 = improve efficiency | <0.5 = structural problem. Caveat: Use trailing-quarter spend in denominator (lag accounts for sales cycle). GM-adjust to avoid overstating efficiency in high-COGS products.

LTV : CAC
LTV = (ARPA × GM%) ÷ Churn Rate
CAC = Total S&M Spend ÷ New Customers Acquired

Benchmarks: >3:1 = minimum viable | >5:1 = strong | >7:1 = elite. Inclusion: ARPA is monthly average revenue per account. Caveat: Churn rate as denominator makes LTV extremely sensitive to small changes in churn — 2% vs. 3% monthly churn = 50% LTV difference.

Net Revenue Retention (NRR)
(Starting ARR + Expansion - Churn - Contraction) ÷ Starting ARR

Benchmarks: >125% = elite | >110% = strong | 100–110% = healthy | <100% = leaky bucket. Caveat: NRR >100% does NOT mean you're growing — it means existing customers expand faster than they churn. You still need new logos to grow ARR.

CAC Payback Period
CAC ÷ (Monthly ARPA × Gross Margin %)

Benchmarks: <6 months = excellent | <12 months = good | <18 months = viable | >24 months = high-risk growth. Caveat: Must segment by Enterprise/MM/SMB — enterprise naturally has longer payback due to higher CAC and longer sales cycles. Comparing blended to benchmarks is misleading.

3x Pipeline Coverage
Total Open Pipeline ÷ Remaining Quota
Threshold = 1 ÷ Historical Win Rate

The 3x is a proxy: At 33% win rate, 3x = 1.0 expected. At 40% win rate, 2.5x is sufficient. At 20% win rate, 5x is required. Caveat: Gross coverage includes phantom pipeline (overdue + stale). Always compare to Clean Coverage = gross minus hygiene-flagged deals.

Forecast Accuracy (MAE%)
MAE% = |Called Value - Actual| ÷ Actual × 100
Bias = (Called - Actual) ÷ Actual × 100

Benchmarks: ±5% at week 8 = top quartile | ±10% = good | ±15% = average. Bias vs. accuracy: MAE measures precision; bias measures systematic direction. A region can be accurate (low MAE) but consistently biased (always calls high). Both signals matter for calibration.

SaaS Quick Ratio
(New MRR + Expansion MRR) ÷ (Churned MRR + Contraction MRR)

Benchmarks: >4.0 = high-growth | 2.0–4.0 = solid | <2.0 = concerning. Interpretation: Unlike NRR, Quick Ratio includes new logo MRR in the numerator — it measures the quality of total growth, not just expansion. A company growing at Quick Ratio 1.5 is barely keeping up with churn.

Commit Accuracy Rate
Deals forecast as Commit that Closed ÷ Total Commit Deals Called

Benchmarks: >90% = excellent | >80% = good | <70% = commit definition is broken. Caveat: Low commit accuracy doesn't always mean sandbagging — it can mean the commit category definition varies by rep. Standardizing commit criteria is a prerequisite for accuracy measurement.

Rep Productivity (Ramp Multiplier)
Avg Bookings (Month N) ÷ (Full Ramp Quota ÷ 12)
Effective Capacity = Σ (Heads × Multiplier)

Typical curve: M1: 5% | M3: 22% | M6: 62% | M9: 90% | M12: 100%. Why it matters: Total headcount ≠ productive capacity. At 25% of staff ramping, effective capacity is ~20% lower than nominal. Ignoring ramp creates systematic capacity planning errors.

DASHBOARD 01
Pipeline Intelligence
Push/pull dynamics · Actuals pacing · Funnel conversion · Velocity decomposition
▶ HTML Demo ⌥ Source

Problem Statement

Push-outs are the #1 source of quarterly forecast misses in enterprise B2B sales. The challenge isn't detecting them — it's distinguishing systemic push-outs (broken process, wrong ICP, single-threaded deals) from one-off pushes (macro headwind, champion leave). Treating them the same leads to wrong interventions.

This tool was built to answer: Which of our pipeline movements are signals vs. noise, and how do they combine to form the actuals pacing curve?

Analytical Framework

Pipeline Velocity Model (4 levers):
Velocity = (# Opps × ACV × Win Rate) ÷ Sales Cycle Days

Any velocity degradation traces to exactly one (or more) levers. This decomposition makes coaching specific rather than generic. "Win rate dropped" → qualification problem. "Cycle lengthened" → multi-threading failure.

Question: Which opportunities moved close dates this week, and in which direction? SQL 
-- Weekly pipeline snapshot with push-out / pull-in detection
-- Multi-snapshot architecture: idempotent, reprocessable, no CRM audit log dependency

WITH weekly_snapshot AS (
  SELECT
    opportunity_id,
    account_name,
    seller_email,
    region,
    segment,
    stage,
    arr_usd,
    close_date,
    DATE_TRUNC('week', updated_at) AS snapshot_week,
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
      PARTITION BY opportunity_id, DATE_TRUNC('week', updated_at)
      ORDER BY updated_at DESC
    ) AS rn
  FROM opportunities
  WHERE is_deleted = FALSE
    AND stage NOT IN ('Closed Won', 'Closed Lost')
),
deduped AS (
  SELECT * FROM weekly_snapshot WHERE rn = 1
)
SELECT
  d.opportunity_id,
  d.account_name,
  d.arr_usd,
  d.close_date           AS current_close,
  p.close_date           AS prior_close,
  CASE
    WHEN d.close_date > p.close_date THEN 'Push-Out'
    WHEN d.close_date < p.close_date THEN 'Pull-In'
    ELSE                                      'No Change'
  END                      AS movement_type,
  d.close_date - p.close_date AS days_moved
FROM deduped d
LEFT JOIN deduped p
  ON  d.opportunity_id = p.opportunity_id
  AND p.snapshot_week  = d.snapshot_week - INTERVAL '1 week'
WHERE d.snapshot_week = DATE_TRUNC('week', CURRENT_DATE)
Question: What is pipeline velocity per day by segment, and which lever is degrading? SQL 
-- Four-lever velocity decomposition by segment and quarter
SELECT
  segment,
  fiscal_quarter,
  COUNT(DISTINCT opportunity_id)                                  AS num_opps,
  ROUND(AVG(arr_usd), 0)                                           AS avg_acv,
  ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN outcome = 'Won' THEN 1.0 ELSE 0 END)
    / NULLIF(COUNT(*), 0), 3)                                    AS win_rate,
  ROUND(AVG(sales_cycle_days), 0)                                  AS avg_cycle_days,
  ROUND( /* Velocity = Volume × ACV × Win Rate ÷ Cycle */
    COUNT(DISTINCT opportunity_id)
    * AVG(arr_usd)
    * (SUM(CASE WHEN outcome = 'Won' THEN 1.0 ELSE 0 END)
       / NULLIF(COUNT(*), 0))
    / NULLIF(AVG(sales_cycle_days), 0), 0
  )                                                                  AS velocity_per_day
FROM closed_opportunities
WHERE close_date >= CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL '365 days'
GROUP BY 1, 2
ORDER BY 2 DESC, velocity_per_day DESC

// Push-out ARR this week — used in the weekly WoW summary tile
Push_Out_Value =
CALCULATE(

1
Multi-snapshot over audit logs

Most CRMs don't maintain granular history at the weekly level. Snapshots are idempotent (can be reprocessed), transparent (queryable), and replayable. Audit logs are append-only and often incomplete after data migrations.

2
Separate push-out table from main pipeline

Isolation avoids circular DAX dependency (Pipeline_Pushed references Pipeline). It also allows independent refresh schedules — pushouts are a derived calculation, not a CRM-updated entity.

3
sourceColumn without brackets for M import tables

In TMDL, [ColumnName] brackets in sourceColumn indicate calculated table references. Import tables use unbracketed column names. Getting this wrong silently fails at model load time.

Is our push-out problem systemic or a few bad actors?
Concentration analysis: if top 3 sellers = 60% of push-outs, it's a person problem. If evenly distributed, it's a process problem.
Which lever in our velocity formula is degrading most?
Four-lever decomposition identifies whether to focus on qualification (win rate), pricing (ACV), prospecting (volume), or process (cycle).
Are we on track to hit quota at current pacing?
Actuals pacing chart shows QTD actuals vs. required weekly run rate to hit plan.
⚠ Limitation: Root cause attribution
Push-out root cause (buyer vs. seller vs. macro) requires CRM field discipline. Without structured reason codes, classification defaults to rule-based heuristics.

Question: Does this deal qualify as Net New, or is it an Installed Base expansion? SQL 
-- Illustrative SQLite query: example policy, not a compensation rule.
-- History is evaluated BEFORE each deal; a deal cannot classify itself.
WITH prior_revenue AS (
  SELECT d.opportunity_id,
         MAX(h.close_date) AS prior_close_date
  FROM closed_won_deals AS d
  LEFT JOIN closed_won_deals AS h
    ON h.account_id = d.account_id
   AND h.close_date < d.close_date
   AND h.opportunity_id <> d.opportunity_id
  GROUP BY d.opportunity_id
)
SELECT d.opportunity_id, d.account_name, d.arr_usd,
       CASE
         WHEN p.prior_close_date IS NULL THEN 'Net New'
         WHEN p.prior_close_date < date(d.close_date, '-5 years')
           THEN 'Net New'
         WHEN p.prior_close_date <= date(d.close_date, '-3 years')
           THEN 'Reactivation'
         ELSE 'Installed Base'
       END AS classification
FROM closed_won_deals AS d
JOIN prior_revenue AS p USING (opportunity_id);
-- Same-day deals need a deterministic timestamp/order policy before use.
Question: What is our GTM efficiency — are we scaling productively or burning capital? SQL 
-- GM-adjusted Magic Number by segment and acquisition channel
-- Why GM-adjusted: raw Magic Number overstates efficiency for high-COGS products
-- Formula: (Net New ARR × Gross Margin %) / Prior Quarter S&M Spend

WITH bookings AS (
  SELECT fiscal_quarter, segment, channel,
         SUM(arr_usd) AS new_arr
  FROM closed_won_deals
  WHERE classification IN ('Net New', 'Reactivation')
  GROUP BY 1, 2, 3
),
spend AS (
  SELECT fiscal_quarter, segment, channel,
         SUM(spend_usd) AS sm_spend
  FROM sales_marketing_spend
  GROUP BY 1, 2, 3
)
SELECT
  b.fiscal_quarter,
  b.segment,
  b.channel,
  ROUND((b.new_arr * 0.72) / NULLIF(s.sm_spend, 0), 2) AS magic_number,
  CASE
    WHEN (b.new_arr*0.72)/NULLIF(s.sm_spend,0) > 1.0  THEN 'Scale aggressively'
    WHEN (b.new_arr*0.72)/NULLIF(s.sm_spend,0) > 0.75 THEN 'Grow thoughtfully'
    WHEN (b.new_arr*0.72)/NULLIF(s.sm_spend,0) > 0.5  THEN 'Improve efficiency'
    ELSE                                                          'Fix GTM before hiring'
  END AS signal
FROM bookings b
LEFT JOIN spend s
  ON b.segment = s.segment
  AND b.channel = s.channel
  AND b.fiscal_quarter = s.fiscal_quarter + INTERVAL '1 quarter'
ORDER BY b.fiscal_quarter DESC, magic_number DESC
Is our Magic Number above 0.75 — should we keep hiring?
Segmented by Enterprise/MM/SMB and by channel. The insight emerges in cross-tabs: Enterprise Inbound 1.2, SMB Outbound 0.2 — different answers for different motions.
Are newer acquisition cohorts expanding at higher NRR rates?
NRR Cohort Grid shows Month 0-24 expansion by acquisition quarter. Rising rows = ICP targeting improvement over time.
Where do we have whitespace vs. saturation in our addressable market?
TAM penetration heatmap: current logos / total ICP accounts by segment × vertical. Guides where to invest in coverage.
⚠ Limitation: Real-time classification
Classification runs at deal close, not prospecting stage. Deals that convert to IB mid-cycle require re-classification logic. Requires CRM field discipline on account deduplication.

DASHBOARD 03
Pipeline Pulse — Revenue Predictability Engine
3x coverage methodology · Forecast accuracy · Clean vs. phantom pipeline · CRO-level reporting
▶ HTML Demo ⌥ Source

Problem Statement

Pipeline coverage tells you how much pipeline you have. It says nothing about whether the forecast is trustworthy. Two teams can both show 3x coverage — one lands within 5% of actual every quarter, the other misses by 20%. The difference is forecast maturity, and it's invisible without accuracy history.

The 3x Rule — First Principles

The 3x threshold is a proxy, not a constant:
Required Coverage = 1 / Historical Win Rate

At 40% win rate, 2.5x is sufficient. At 20%, you need 5x. A dynamic threshold beats the static rule every time — it forces the analytical conversation instead of hiding behind a heuristic.

Question: How accurate were our weekly forecast calls vs. what actually closed? SQL 
-- Forecast accuracy scorecard: called vs. actual, by region and week of quarter
-- Bias detection: regions that consistently call high or low need calibration factors

WITH forecast_log AS (
  SELECT
    fiscal_quarter,
    WEEKOFYEAR(forecast_date)
      - WEEKOFYEAR(DATE_TRUNC('quarter', forecast_date)) + 1  AS week_of_quarter,
    region,
    forecast_type,  -- 'Commit', 'Best Case', 'Weighted'
    forecast_value
  FROM forecast_calls
),
actuals AS (
  SELECT fiscal_quarter, region, SUM(arr_usd) AS actual_bookings
  FROM closed_won_deals GROUP BY 1, 2
)
SELECT
  f.fiscal_quarter,
  f.week_of_quarter,
  f.region,
  f.forecast_value                                      AS called,
  a.actual_bookings                                      AS actual,
  ABS(f.forecast_value - a.actual_bookings)
    / NULLIF(a.actual_bookings, 0)                      AS abs_error_pct,
  CASE
    WHEN f.forecast_value > a.actual_bookings THEN 'High (optimistic)'
    WHEN f.forecast_value < a.actual_bookings THEN 'Low (conservative)'
    ELSE                                             'Accurate'
  END                                                    AS bias_direction
FROM forecast_log f
JOIN actuals a USING (fiscal_quarter, region)
WHERE f.forecast_type = 'Commit'
ORDER BY 1 DESC, 2, 3
DAX: Hygiene-adjusted "clean" coverage vs. gross coverage DAX 
// Clean Coverage excludes overdue and stale pipeline (phantom pipeline)
// Gross coverage 4.1x with 30% phantom = Clean coverage 2.9x — a very different story
Clean_Coverage_Ratio =
VAR TotalPipeline =
    CALCULATE(SUM(Pipeline[ARR_USD]), Pipeline[Is_Open] = TRUE())
VAR PhantomPipeline =
    CALCULATE(
        SUM(Pipeline[ARR_USD]),
        Pipeline[Is_Open] = TRUE(),
        OR(
            Pipeline[Is_Overdue] = TRUE(),
            Pipeline[Days_Since_Last_Activity] > 14
        )
    )
VAR CleanPipeline   = TotalPipeline - PhantomPipeline
VAR Quota           = SUM(Bookings_Actuals[AOP_Bookings])
RETURN
    DIVIDE(CleanPipeline, Quota)


// Stage-weighted probabilistic forecast — independent of rep commit category
Stage_Weighted_Forecast =
SUMX(
    Pipeline,
    Pipeline[ARR_USD] *
    SWITCH(Pipeline[Stage],
        "Qualify",   0.10,
        "Develop",   0.20,
        "Validate",  0.35,
        "Propose",   0.50,
        "Negotiate", 0.72,
        "Commit",    0.88,
        0
    )
)

Forecast Trust Score: Aurora has a 71% commit accuracy rate and +11% systematic bias (calls high). Discount Aurora commits by 9% in the global rollup.
Is our 3x coverage actually 3x, or inflated by stale deals?
Clean Coverage metric isolates phantom pipeline (overdue + stale). Gross 4.1x may actually be 2.9x clean — a 29% overstatement.
What does the pipeline model say we should close this quarter?
Stage-weighted predicted close provides a third forecast line alongside Commit (rep-reported) and Quota — where all three converge is where the CRO should commit.

Forecast accuracy tracking requires a historical log of weekly calls. If the org doesn't currently capture this, the accuracy scorecard needs 2-3 quarters of new data before it becomes meaningful.

Problem Statement

Quota attainment % answers "did they hit quota?" It doesn't answer: Was the quota calibrated correctly? Is the distribution shape healthy? Are ramping reps on track? What happens to capacity if 20% of reps leave? And critically: are some reps closing deals that churn within 12 months — hitting quota while destroying NRR?

SPM Framework

  • Calibration: Is quota design sound? TAM-adjusted or peanut butter?
  • Distribution: Is the attainment histogram unimodal (healthy) or bimodal (broken comp)?
  • Capacity: How does headcount translate to productive quota-carrying capacity?
  • Quality: Are reps closing deals that expand, or deals that churn?
Question: How does rep productivity evolve by tenure month, and what are our ramp multipliers? SQL 
-- Ramp curve analysis: productivity multiplier by tenure month
-- Output feeds the capacity planning model (headcount × multiplier = effective quota capacity)

SELECT
  DATEDIFF('month', hire_date, close_date) AS tenure_month,
  segment,
  COUNT(DISTINCT seller_id)                AS rep_count,
  ROUND(AVG(arr_closed), 0)               AS avg_monthly_bookings,
  ROUND(AVG(attainment_pct), 3)           AS avg_attainment,
  ROUND(                                    -- Normalized multiplier (1.0 = fully ramped)
    AVG(arr_closed)
    / NULLIF(AVG(full_ramp_quota / 12.0), 0),
  3) AS productivity_multiplier
FROM rep_performance_monthly
WHERE hire_date >= CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL '3 years'
  AND tenure_month BETWEEN 0 AND 18
GROUP BY 1, 2
ORDER BY 2, 1
Question: Is our attainment distribution healthy (unimodal) or does it signal a broken quota design (bimodal)? SQL 
-- Attainment histogram with bimodal detection flag
-- A bimodal pattern (clusters at <50% AND >120%) = broken quota design or comp plan

SELECT
  fiscal_quarter,
  segment,
  CASE
    WHEN attainment_pct < 0.50  THEN '0–50%'
    WHEN attainment_pct < 0.70  THEN '50–70%'
    WHEN attainment_pct < 0.90  THEN '70–90%'
    WHEN attainment_pct < 1.00  THEN '90–100%'
    WHEN attainment_pct < 1.20  THEN '100–120%'
    ELSE                               '120%+'
  END                                  AS band,
  COUNT(*)                             AS rep_count,
  ROUND(COUNT(*) * 100.0
    / SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER (
        PARTITION BY fiscal_quarter, segment
      ), 1)                            AS pct_of_reps
FROM rep_attainment
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3
ORDER BY 1 DESC, 2, 3
How many productive AEs do we actually have vs. total headcount?
Capacity model: Fully ramped × 1.0 + M4-6 × 0.5 + M1-3 × 0.1 = effective capacity. Often 20-30% lower than nominal headcount during growth phases.
Are some reps hitting quota by closing deals that churn in 12 months?
NRR attribution by AE sourcing cohort: quality-adjusted attainment. A rep at 120% whose logos churn at 80% GRR is a liability, not a top performer.
Is our quota design calibrated correctly across territories?
Attainment histogram shape analysis. Unimodal (good). Bimodal — concentration at <50% AND >120% — signals territories with unfair quota allocation.
⚠ Limitation: Territory quality normalization
The capacity model assumes equal quota per headcount. Real-world TAM-adjusted quota design requires firmographic data per territory to normalize. Without it, attainment reflects territory quality as much as rep skill.

Python: How is AI narrative context built — specifically, what WoW changes get injected into the prompt? Python 
# WoW deal context builder — injected into LLM narrative prompt
# Supplies evidence for validation; does not guarantee AI factual accuracy

def build_deal_changes_context(region: str, quarter: str, week: int) -> dict:
    """
    Computes deal-level delta between current and prior week submission.
    Returns structured context for the LLM narrative generation prompt.
    """
    current  = fetch_submissions(region, quarter, week)
    prior    = fetch_submissions(region, quarter, week - 1)

    # Match deals by ID, classify each change type
    prior_ids = {d['id'] for d in prior}
    added     = [d for d in current if d['id'] not in prior_ids]
    removed   = [d for d in prior   if d['id'] not in {c['id'] for c in current}]
    changed   = [
        {'deal': c, 'delta': c['amount'] - p['amount']}
        for c in current
        for p in prior
        if  c['id'] == p['id']
        and abs(c['amount'] - p['amount']) > 100_000  # threshold: >100K change
    ]

    # Reconcile ALL movements; significance only controls narrative selection.
    net = (sum(d['amount'] for d in current)
           - sum(d['amount'] for d in prior))

    return {
        'region':         region,
        'week':           week,
        'net_impact_M':   round(net / 1_000_000, 1),

Self-contained single-file architecture

The portal is one HTML file with no external CDN dependencies. It opens in any browser with no server. This was a deliberate governance decision — the file is portable, auditable, and can be shared directly. This portfolio file is an interactive mockup. No deployment history, incident record, or production release count is established by its version label.

2
Context injection over generic prompts

The Python example computes deal-level changes for a possible narrative context. A future integration would need source references, numerical validation and human review. Providing context alone does not guarantee a correct narrative.

3
Quarter-locked state machine per submission

A production design would enforce approval transitions on the server and retain an immutable audit trail. The current static prototype does not implement these protections; CSS cannot enforce authorization.

Roadmap

What I Would Build Next

Every tool in this portfolio is intentionally bounded. These are the extensions that would move each dashboard from strong to exceptional.

📊 Pipeline Intelligence

Predictive slippage model using gradient boosting on: stage age vs. P75 benchmark, push history count, multi-threading depth (contacts engaged), next-step recency. Output: probability-of-slip score per open deal, surfaced proactively 3 weeks before quarter close.

🎯 Net New Tracker

Close the feedback loop: connect ICP fit score at acquisition to 12-month NRR outcome. Run a correlation regression to validate (or invalidate) the ICP model. High-ICP logos should show >115% NRR; low-ICP logos should show <95%. This makes the model auditable and continuously improvable.

⚡ Pipeline Pulse

Dynamic 3x threshold using Bayesian updating. Instead of a fixed line, the required coverage threshold updates weekly using the current-quarter-to-date win rate and remaining selling days. The model converges to the actual close rate as the quarter progresses — far more accurate than the static heuristic.

🏆 Sales League

Rep survival analysis using Kaplan-Meier estimator. Input features: tenure month, consecutive quarters below 50%, manager assignment, territory quality score. Output: 90-day attrition probability per rep. Flags at-risk reps before they resign, enabling preemptive coaching or backfill planning.

📋 WCI Portal

Replace simulated AI with production LLM integration via API (streaming output). Add vector memory: narratives from prior weeks are retrieved and used as context to ensure consistency and progression across submissions.
